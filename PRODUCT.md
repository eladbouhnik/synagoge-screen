# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Congregants in a synagogue sanctuary or lobby, viewing a fullscreen TV/kiosk mounted on a wall from several meters away, often during or between prayers. Many are elderly. Attention is split — they glance at the screen, not stare at it. A second audience is the shul's gabbai/admin who configures screens via the `/admin` panel (separate product surface).

## Product Purpose

Display live synagogue information at a glance: prayer times (tfilot), daily halachic times (zmanim), announcements, shiurim schedule, yahrzeit/iluy neshama lists, daily halacha learning, birthdays, donor recognition. Content rotates across configured screens per synagogue (`board_key`). Success = correct time read instantly from across the room, zero ambiguity, zero clutter.

## Brand Personality

Modern, minimal, calm. A well-run institution's screen, not a corporate SaaS product and not ornate religious signage. Confidence through restraint and clarity, not decoration.

## Anti-references

- No kitsch/clipart religious decoration (stock menorahs, gaudy gold borders, ornate frames).
- No generic SaaS dashboard look (card grids, gradient text, hero-metric tiles, glassmorphism) — reads as "AI made this."
- No airport-gate digital-signage sterility.

## Design Principles

- Distance-first legibility: every number and label must resolve instantly from meters away, in a room with variable lighting (bright daylight to dim evening).
- One glance, one fact: each screen shows one clear type of information at a time, no competing hierarchy.
- Quiet confidence: modern and minimal, restrained color, no ornamentation for its own sake.
- Hebrew RTL native: layout, numerals, and typography are designed RTL-first, not adapted from an LTR template.
- Calm under urgency: urgent messages (alerts, red-alert overlay) must escalate visually without breaking the screen's overall composure.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

High-contrast, large-text priority beyond standard WCAG AA — optimized for distance readability and elderly/low-vision congregants. Minimum effective type size and contrast ratio should be validated against real viewing distance, not just AA color-contrast math.
